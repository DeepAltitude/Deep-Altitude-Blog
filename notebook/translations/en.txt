@@ paragliding
# Paragliding
> What does the air teach?

Wait for good wind, good conditions... In unfavourable conditions, it’s better not to fly at all, to rest, read... Have another plan.

After a storm the flying is good... but in bad conditions, better to stay calm.
There are days when the air is active. If you turn well, you’ll climb. If you don’t turn... you’ll either stay at your level, or the rising air passing by will hit the wing and you can have trouble... and if you’ve completely let go of control, collapse, you’ll fall into a tree.

Never complacent.
Always check. Checklists.

Always have plan B, always OK not to fly after arrival.

# Learning languages
> Finding your energy in the process

### Why do I learn languages?

Learning languages opens new horizons, both inside and outside.
It reminds me to be... humble, because when I start learning a language I can’t be perfect. I do something that doesn’t work yet, that’s hard, repeat the basics many times in a row.

Tutors help a lot with consistency.

There will be stories and reflections about these languages:
- Russian
- Spanish
- Danish
- French
- Portuguese
- Italian
- German

### Finding your energy in the process

I think the most important thing in learning a language is to know yourself. Listen to yourself, to your energy levels. And ride that wave, change methods every day if necessary; if you don’t want to study grammar, learn through examples, do drills. If it’s boring, something’s wrong.

### Current method

Lessons with tutors, about six to ten hours a week.
I read or listen to AI-written stories, anything interesting, at an appropriate level.
I print pages of things that interest me so that early in the morning, after meditation and before a lesson, I can quietly read with my phone switched off and underline interesting things with a marker.
Drills, written by AI

@@ expectations
# Don’t expect people to give what they cannot give
> Expectations, reality

A big lesson... not to expect people or organisations to give what they cannot give.

## Lack of leadership

When a conflict arose at work, I watched what was happening. A colleague running wild, who started complaining about everything more and more, lying, arriving late, taking offence at the simplest questions... I waited for strong action from my manager. But even at the very beginning, she had said that she really dislikes conflict and finds difficult conversations hard. Now it was time to set boundaries, time for a difficult conversation. I invited my manager to a meeting, because it’s my professional responsibility to discuss risks to our team’s results, company culture and individual reputations. And... Nothing happened.

On the one hand, I was disappointed because my expectation that my manager would do her job wasn’t met. But I realised something much more valuable: I was waiting for her to do what she had long been saying she dislikes, doesn’t want to do, won’t do.

I mistakenly expected something from a person who isn’t capable of it. The lesson is to really trust my own judgement of people, to admit to myself that everyone has limits to their abilities, and many people in higher positions do not have the skills that, in my opinion, they ought to have.

## Career progress

I heard from others that you can’t build a career in this company. Many of the owners’ family members sit comfortably up high, and most educated employees chill, do a bit of work and that’s it. Relax in the office. The company offers security and stability, and the price is stagnation. Salaries aren’t too bad, you don’t overwork... But there are no prospects, no leadership, no good examples to learn from.

Human Resources said in a conversation that unfortunately, however regrettable, the company hasn’t yet developed stable career-development processes, and the owner’s son dislikes applying the label “Talent”, so we have no talent development.
